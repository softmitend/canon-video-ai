# Scene 01 Audio — Nightmare Awakening

Audio canon untuk scene `prompts/scenes/nightmare-awakening.md`.

Target durasi: **15–22 detik**.

## AUDIO DIRECTION

Scene ini sebaiknya tidak memakai narator yang terus berbicara. Tension akan lebih kuat jika audio didominasi oleh **breathing, room tone, rain, cloth movement, dan thunder**, dengan voice hanya sangat sedikit bila diperlukan.

Overall mood: intimate psychological tension, realistic nighttime ambience, restrained fear, no melodramatic horror stingers, no exaggerated anime vocalizations.

---

# MASTER SFX PROMPT

```text
Create a cinematic, realistic sound design for a 15–22 second psychological anime scene set in a clean minimalist bedroom at midnight during a violent rainstorm.

The audio should feel intimate and close, with realistic spatial depth and restrained psychological tension rather than loud horror clichés.

Primary ambience:
- heavy rain striking a large bedroom window continuously
- low distant wind outside
- occasional deep rolling thunder
- subtle room tone from a quiet modern bedroom
- faint rain resonance through glass and walls

Character Foley:
- soft uneven sleeping breaths at the beginning
- breathing gradually becoming tense and shallow during the nightmare
- faint movement of hair and pillow fabric
- subtle satin pajama and bedsheet rustling
- a sudden sharp inhale/gasp when she wakes
- quick bedding movement as she abruptly sits upright
- heavy, controlled breathing after waking
- soft hand and sleeve movement as both hands rise toward her face
- subtle skin and fabric friction as she roughly rubs her face with both hands
- breathing slowly stabilizing afterward

Storm progression:
- keep rain continuous throughout the scene
- distant thunder may appear early but should remain subdued
- as she notices the window, the rain becomes more perceptually present in the mix
- near the final shot, add a sudden bright lightning event followed by a powerful but realistic thunder crack with a short natural delay

Mixing direction:
- close character breathing in the foreground
- rain located spatially toward the window side of the room
- bedroom ambience low and quiet
- avoid overwhelming the breathing with rain
- use dynamic contrast so the final thunder feels powerful because earlier sounds remain restrained
- cinematic stereo width, realistic indoor reflections, subtle low-frequency thunder energy
- no music unless added separately
- no jump-scare stingers
- no supernatural whispers
- no screaming
- no cartoon sound effects
- no exaggerated heartbeat unless extremely subtle and almost subliminal

The emotional arc should move from uneasy sleep, to mounting fear, to sudden panic, to exhausted disbelief, and finally to quiet ominous attention toward the storm.
```

# CHARACTER VOICE LOCK

Use this whenever the character speaks in this project.

```text
Adult female voice, early twenties.
Soft and naturally feminine, but not childish, breathy-cute, high-pitched, or bubbly.
Low-to-mid feminine register with a smooth, clear tone.
Quiet confidence and subtle authority underneath the softness.
Controlled articulation, intelligent and composed presence.
Emotion should come through micro-changes in breath, pacing, and vocal tension rather than theatrical delivery.
Natural conversational voice, cinematic and intimate.
No exaggerated anime voice acting.
No squeaky tone.
No overly seductive delivery.
No dramatic sobbing.
No artificial vocal fry.

For this scene specifically, her voice begins physically tense and breathless after waking from a nightmare. She is frightened and disoriented, but actively trying to regain control. Keep the performance restrained, close-mic, slightly shaky at first, then progressively steadier.
```

# NON-VERBAL VOCAL PERFORMANCE PROMPT

Recommended default for this scene. This preserves tension better than adding full narration.

```text
Perform realistic non-verbal vocal reactions for an adult woman waking suddenly from a disturbing nightmare.

Begin with soft sleeping breaths that gradually become uneasy and shallow.
At the exact waking moment, produce one sudden sharp inhale or small involuntary gasp, never a scream.
Immediately afterward, breathe quickly and unevenly for several seconds as adrenaline peaks.
While she rubs her face with both hands, add one restrained frustrated exhale through the nose and mouth.
As she looks toward the stormy window, her breathing becomes slower and quieter but remains slightly tense.

Keep every reaction subtle, human, intimate, and cinematic.
No crying.
No whimpering.
No spoken words.
No exaggerated gasps.
No anime-style vocalization.
```

# OPTIONAL INTERNAL VOICE OVER

**Default canon recommendation: do not use voice-over unless the story needs her thoughts communicated explicitly.** The scene is stronger when the viewer reads her fear through breathing and body language.

If internal VO is needed, use only one short line after she has rubbed her face and before she fully focuses on the window.

## Neutral version

Script:

```text
"...What was that?"
```

Performance prompt:

```text
Deliver the line as an internal thought rather than spoken dialogue.
Adult female voice, low-to-mid register, intimate close-mic tone.
She has just awakened from a frightening nightmare and is still catching her breath.
The line should sound genuinely unsettled and confused, but controlled.
Very soft volume, slightly delayed pacing, almost whispered internally without becoming airy or cute.
A tiny pause before the words.
No melodrama, no crying, no theatrical fear.
```

## More disturbed version

Script:

```text
"Why did it feel so real?"
```

Use only if the story canon intends the nightmare to feel unusually significant.

Performance prompt:

```text
Internal monologue from an adult woman trying to rationalize a nightmare that felt disturbingly real.
Speak quietly and slowly, still slightly breathless.
Soft feminine voice with restrained fear beneath a normally composed and confident personality.
Emphasize "real" very slightly, without dramatic acting.
The emotion is private unease, not panic.
```

# AUDIO MIX PRIORITY

Use this hierarchy when assembling the final scene:

1. Character breath / waking gasp
2. Rain against window
3. Bedsheet, satin pajama, hand and body Foley
4. Thunder and distant wind
5. Optional internal VO
6. Music only if later required by the full film score

The storm should support the character, not bury her. During the waking gasp and face-rub Foley, slightly duck the rain rather than making every layer equally loud.

## Canon choice for Scene 01

Preferred version: **no conventional narration, no dialogue, only non-verbal character vocals + environmental SFX.** If a thought line is necessary, use only one short internal line.
